What the record says

The ITU-T register identifies The Open Group as the sender of liaison statement s260, approved on 10 November 2026. The entry describes it as an invitation for feedback on O-DXA for Government and gives a reply deadline of 31 March 2027. The approval date refers to the liaison statement. The register documents a request for feedback from ITU-T groups. It does not establish that a standard has been adopted or that organisations outside the listed groups can take part.

What architects can do with it

Poland has the State Information Architecture model, known as AIP. EIRA supports the description and design of interoperable public services in Europe. A team comparing these approaches with O-DXA could start with a single example. Imagine a service where one agency confirms a person's eligibility and another uses that information to decide an application.

Three questions are enough for a first comparison:

  1. Who owns the decision and the data? Where does each model identify the service owner, the data owner and the authority accountable for the eligibility confirmation?
  2. What can be reused? Is eligibility confirmation defined as a shared capability? How are the exchange contract and its exceptions described?
  3. How is conformance verified? Which artefact records the architecture decision, and what evidence shows that the service works across agencies after deployment?

Editorial takeaway. Start with a short mapping and a list of open questions. As further O-DXA material becomes available, the mapping will make it easier to see which concepts correspond to existing models.
